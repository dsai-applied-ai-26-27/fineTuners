Before you apply

How a course gets written. The content is plain text files in a fixed format, written on your own computer and handed in on the site as one file. Nothing is typed into the website. You can write the files by hand, or with a free chatbot, and for a lecture or two that works. For a whole course the realistic way is a paid AI assistant that can read the slides and write files on your computer (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or similar, about 20 € a month). You hand it a lecture, it writes in the site's format from the instructions in the repository, and you check what it wrote against the lecture.

What stays with you. The checking. What goes up under your course has to be right, and an assistant gets things wrong with a straight face. Plan an hour or two per lecture.

What happens next. The person who runs the site reads your application and contacts you on the number you give. If it is a yes, the site shows you how writing a course works, start to finish (the guide is there to read now), and from then on it tells you about your course, under the bell at the top of the page and, where e-mail is set up, by e-mail: that you were accepted, and when the tools you work with have a new version.
